The pipeline

  1. Read: Gemini vision transcribes the pack text into a fixed schema (brand, composition as printed, legibility). It has no field for opinions and is told never to fill in ingredients from memory.
  2. Confirm: you check every field against the pack. Nothing reaches the database before this step.
  3. Match: brand to catalogue ({{ kb.brands|length }} Indian brands, stored in the database). Ingredient names are recognised only by exact name, listed synonym or salt-stripping. Fuzzy matches are suggestions you must accept.
  4. Check: the deterministic rule engine below runs on confirmed entries only.
  5. Review: a pharmacist summary with every flag, its evidence and its source.

Deliberate limits

  • No dose suggestions, no "safe" verdicts, and never advice to start, stop or switch a medicine. Every message ends in "review with a pharmacist".
  • Daily-amount checks only add up what you entered per day and compare the total with the label maximum: a fixed limit for adults, and for children under 12 a weight-based limit (mg per kg per day) or the label's age band. A child with no weight recorded is shown as not checked, never as safe.
  • The adult ibuprofen limit is the over-the-counter label limit (1,200 mg a day). A doctor may prescribe more; the message says so.
  • Low-dose aspirin (under 300 mg) is not counted as an NSAID pain reliever, but it still counts as an antiplatelet in the bleeding rule.
  • The Beers rule checks only antihistamines named explicitly in the 2023 AGS Beers Criteria. Pheniramine is not named there, so it is not flagged.
  • If a strength is unreadable, conditional classes are applied conservatively (more review, not less).
  • Photos are held in memory until you confirm or discard, then dropped. Only confirmed text fields are stored.
  • Rule wording is a prototype pending pharmacist review. Sources are cited for traceability, not as clinical validation.
